Back in middle school, the infamous COVID years took over the world, and my computer consumed me. I browsed the Art of Problem Solving forums and got really into problem writing. I joined a few teams, all now dead, but surprisingly, six years later, I still keep in touch with many, and I have even met a few in-person! The big ones I joined were the Ze Mathematics Competitions, Junior Mathematicians' Problem-Solving Contest, and the XYZ Mathematics Contest. Most of these died the moment I entered high school, unfortunately, as many of the leaders graduated.

When I first began writing problems, I was terrible. I don't think any of my problems got selected unless I was the person deciding what made it onto the test. Over time, however, I got a lot better. I still am not perfect-nobody is-but I am quite satisfied with how many of them turned out. My biggest recommendation is: surround yourself with a team of people and take the criticism. Learn from them about styling and quality; think about what makes a problem nice to you. Many of the people I worked with made some of the best mocks on Art of Problem Solving to date!

Now, when I came to high school, I got involved with the Florida Student Association of Mathematics, and I am really thankful for that. During my freshman year, I was just a new member of the team. I never found much success at the competitions, but I generally made the team. And so, in 10th grade, I was part of the Curriculum Committee. I am really thankful for the experience I had in middle school and all the people I learned from. The following year, my experience from way back earned me the Chair of the Curriculum Committee.

Special shoutouts to: AW (USA), BG (USA), EL (USA), JL (USA), JM (USA), KY (USA), MS (IND), SD (IND), SC (USA). You all were incredible and taught me so much!
